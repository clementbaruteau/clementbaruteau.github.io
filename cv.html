Selection of 5 recent publications:

  • 1Dhouib, Baruteau, Mathis et al. 2024, A&A, 682, A85 — link

    summary: The radial migration of Jupiter's satellites, informed by over a century of astrometric data, allows to estimate the dissipation of internal waves in Jupiter due to its satellites. This dissipation proves to be ~two orders of magnitude lower than that obtained in our hydrodynamical simulations of tidal interactions, which use the most recent model of Jupiter's interior, thereby highlighting the need to advance the modeling of tides.

  • 2Debras, Baruteau & Donati 2021, MNRAS, 500, 1621 — link

    summary: Through long-term 2D hydrodynamical simulations, we showed that a giant planet could acquire high eccentricity (~0.4) after migrating into a cavity within its protoplanetary disc. We argued that this scenario could represent a generic mechanism for increasing the eccentricity of giant planets within their natal discs and explain the high median eccentricity (~0.25) of warm Jupiters.

  • 3Baruteau et al. 2021, MNRAS, 505, 359 — link

    summary: Follow-up of the Debras et al. (2021) paper, in which simulations results were post-processed by radiative transfer calculations to produce synthetic observables of discs where a giant planet becomes eccentric after having migrated inside a cavity in the disc. The eccentricity of the planet makes the gas also eccentric inside the cavity, where the CO emission takes the form of large asymmetries detectable with ALMA.

  • 4Wafflard-Fernandez & Baruteau 2020, MNRAS, 493, 5892 — link

    summary: A giant planet tends to migrate intermittently toward its star when its protoplanetary disc is still massive. During each phase where migration slows down, the planet triggers the formation of dust-trapping pressure maximum just beyond its orbit. A single giant planet migrating intermittently through its disc can thus create a series of dark and bright rings in the dust continuum emission, which is detectable with ALMA.

  • 5Baruteau et al. 2019, MNRAS, 486, 304 — link

    summary: The dust continuum emission in the MWC 758 disc exhibits structures suggesting the presence of companions in the disc (spirals, asymmetric and eccentric rings). Via hydrodynamical simulations of disc-planets interactions post-processed by dust radiative transfer calculations, we showed that these structures can be explained by the presence of two giant planets in the disc at ~35 and ~140 AU from the star.
